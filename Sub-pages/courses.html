Here is a curated list of the most important courses I've taken, with descriptions and how they've helped further my understanding of key engineering physics concepts.
Course Name Description Key Skills
ENPH353 Autonomous robotics design focused on building a full stack system: embedded control, sensing, and perception. Emphasis on turning noisy real-world signals into reliable robot behavior under time constraints. Embedded C/C++, FreeRTOS, PCB design, sensors/actuators, control loops, computer vision, debugging & integration
MATH255 Differential equations for modeling physical systems. Covered solving ODEs and interpreting solutions as system behavior (transients, steady-state, stability) across mechanical and electrical examples. ODE solving methods, Laplace transforms, stability intuition, modeling physical systems, linear systems
MATH257 Partial differential equations with a focus on how field equations arise from conservation laws. Learned standard solution techniques and how to translate PDE results into physical predictions. PDEs (heat/wave/Laplace), separation of variables, Fourier series, boundary/initial conditions, physical interpretation
MECH280 Introduction to fluid mechanics from first principles through applications. Built intuition for pressure/velocity relationships and learned when simplified models apply versus when viscosity and losses matter. Control volumes, Bernoulli + losses, dimensional analysis, Navier–Stokes concepts, flow regimes, problem solving
MATH217 Multivariable calculus for analyzing fields and geometry in 2D/3D. Developed the tools used everywhere in physics: gradients, flux, and the integral theorems that connect local and global behavior. Vector calculus, partial derivatives, line/surface integrals, Green/Stokes/Divergence theorems, coordinate transforms
ENPH259 Foundations of circuit analysis and design, bridging theory to measurement and real hardware behavior. Emphasis on building circuits that work outside the ideal assumptions (loading, bandwidth, noise). Circuit analysis, Thevenin/Norton, AC steady-state, op-amp circuits, filters, DAC/ADC concepts, lab measurement
ELEC342 Electromechanical energy conversion and machine fundamentals. Learned how motors and generators behave from equivalent circuits and electromagnetic principles, connecting models to torque-speed and efficiency behavior. Machine modeling, torque-speed curves, efficiency, induction/synchronous basics, equivalent circuits, power flow
PHYS304 Advanced quantum mechanics focused on wave mechanics, operators, and physical interpretation of quantum systems. Emphasis on translating mathematical formalism into measurable predictions for real physical systems. Schrödinger equation, operators & eigenstates, perturbation theory, expectation values, probability amplitudes, problem modeling
CPEN312 Real-time and embedded system design integrating hardware and low-level software. Covered microcontroller architecture, interrupts, scheduling, and reliable system behavior under timing constraints. Embedded C/C++, real-time systems, interrupts & timers, concurrency, hardware/software integration, debugging
PHYS350 Classical mechanics using advanced analytical methods to model complex physical systems. Strong focus on Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalisms, conservation laws, and system dynamics. Lagrangian mechanics, Euler–Lagrange equations, coupled systems, oscillations, energy methods, analytical modeling
