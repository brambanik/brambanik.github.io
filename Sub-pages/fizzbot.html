Fizzbot: Discord Imitation with Minimal Compute

Feburary 13th, 2026

I recently took a course in machine learning, ENPH353. There were a lot of very cool takeaways culminating in an imitation learning model to drive a racecar along an obstacle-filled track. Inspired by this, I had the idea to prepare something for the upcoming Fizz Talks event which was a space for students to share their talents, interests, or creations (which I also happened to organize as the Co-Student Life Coordinator). Fizzbot was the result of this! It's a little GPT model that emulates messages from one of the engineering physics discord servers.

Scrubbing Data

The first step in this project was to gather enough data to train the model. Luckily, discord servers are often humming with activity, which means thousands upon thousands of lines of text to train on. To do this, I found this nifty firefox extension called Discrub that converts contents of servers into easy-to-manipulate JSON files. Unfortunately, the discord server I scrubbed only had ~36,000 lines of text. This sounds like a lot in any context but machine learning. Alas, I only had two days to make this work before presenting it so I made do. After some simple Python to convert the JSON into a legible format for training, I ended up with a masterfile that looked a bit like the following:

Training and Lessons on Tokenization

With all the data in a clean .txt file, I could get to actually training. Alas, I had no experience with GPTs so I turned to one of the legends of artificial intelligence: Andrej Karpathy. By minorly adapting his nano-GPT framework, I had a good starting point to make my own model. I put my text file as the source and started training... and immediately realized two problems. First, I had one CPU to train on (yes, CPU not GPU). It was taking literally ages to train. Second, when I finally got an output, it was completely nonsense.

Fig. 2: First Outputs of Fizzbot

Good luck reading this.

Clearly, I had some work to do. The easiest things to fix were to scale back the size of the model for training purposes. After a lot of tinkering, I ended up with a model that had 6 layers, ran for 1000 iterations, with a batch size of 6. This meant the model took around 2 hours to train which isn't the worst when you're doing homework in the meantime. Yet, this still produced meaningless outputs. I found out quickly this was because of the tokenization I'd been using. One of the options is character-level tokenization, where each character is its own token. This works for low-entropy datasets; Discord messages are most definitely NOT low entropy. So I switched to subword-level tokenization instead, meaning each word was chopped up into a couple bits instead of characters, i.e. Raining -> "Rain" + "ing"

This yielded significantly better results, to the point where it was almost coherent! I can't really ask for more from a tiny model like this. Check out the results below!

Temperature Shenanigans

While screwing around with the Python for the training and running of the model, I noticed this parameter called temperature. After a quick search, I found out that it controls the "randomness" of the GPT. Basically, the model has a list of most probable selections for each subsequent token, and the temperature decides to what degree it will pick the highest probability option. Lower temperature results in a lower entropy model that always picks the "best" option, while higher temperature leads it to pick lower probability predictions. While high temperature outputs were mostly nonsensical, the low temperature outputs gave some insight into what words (and people) the model would latch on to. It was interesting to see people's behaviors encoded into the model, with their most frequent message types being grouped together, as seen below.

The Future of Fizzbot

The biggest improvements I could make to this projects would be increasing the context window of the bot (so it has a more cohesive flow of conversation) and majorly upgrading the size of the model now that I have a dedicated GPU (and a Jetson Nano, which I plan on screwing around with soon!). While I mostly left this project in the dust, some people I knew decided to pick it up and improve it significantly. They even made a bot on our discord server and used a pre-trained GPT to make the outputs very... intelligent? It's really cool to see Fizzbot evolve, but I won't put those updates here because they're not my work.
